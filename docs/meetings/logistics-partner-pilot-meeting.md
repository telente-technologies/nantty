# Nantty × Logistics Partner
## Door-to-door delivery discovery and pilot meeting

**Meeting date:** 26 October 2026  
**Purpose:** Evaluate whether this company can safely and reliably handle a small, measurable pilot of seller-to-buyer deliveries for Nantty.  
**Desired outcome:** Leave with a named operational contact, a written pilot proposal, confirmed service area and pricing, a sample tracking/proof-of-delivery flow, and clear next steps. This is a discovery meeting—not a commitment to exclusivity or a large rollout.

---

## 1. What Nantty needs from a delivery partner

Nantty is a social-commerce marketplace. A buyer places an order from a seller; the goods need to be collected from the seller and delivered to the buyer’s address. The delivery partner should provide dependable pickup, tracking and delivery evidence, predictable charges, exception handling, and a workable reverse-delivery process.

For the pilot, Nantty is evaluating the delivery operation—not asking the courier to hold, release, or reconcile marketplace/escrow funds. Keep delivery charges and goods payments clearly itemized. Do not promise a delivery or payment workflow until both sides have agreed how the order statuses and evidence will be exchanged.

### Opening script

> “Thanks for meeting with me. Nantty is preparing a social-commerce marketplace where customers buy from independent sellers. I’d like to understand your actual coverage and operating process, then see whether we can run a small door-to-door pilot together. We want to start in a limited area, learn from real deliveries, and expand only if service quality and unit economics work. Could you walk me through how an order moves from a seller’s pickup point to a buyer’s door, including what happens when something goes wrong?”

### Suggested agenda (45–60 minutes)

1. Introductions and the partner’s operating model — 5 minutes
2. Coverage, capacity and service levels — 10 minutes
3. End-to-end delivery and exception workflows — 15 minutes
4. Pricing, liability, safety and settlement — 10 minutes
5. Pilot scope, success measures and next steps — 10–20 minutes

## 2. Questions to ask

Ask for concrete examples, current figures, and written evidence where possible. Distinguish what is available today from what is planned or “coming soon.”

### A. Coverage, capacity and service levels

- Which exact cities, districts, neighborhoods, and postal/address zones do you serve today? Can you mark them on a map?
- Which areas are genuinely door-to-door, and which require pickup at a hub or customer collection point?
- Where are your pickup hubs and dispatch teams? What is the typical pickup radius from each hub?
- What delivery options do you operate (same-day, next-day, scheduled, standard)? What are the cutoff times and operating days?
- What are your current median and 90th-percentile pickup-to-delivery times by area and service level? What percentage arrive within the promised window?
- How many orders can you reliably collect and deliver per day in the proposed pilot area? What is the peak-day capacity?
- How do you handle weekends, public holidays, severe weather, traffic disruption, and service-area boundaries?
- Can you share recent performance data for a comparable merchant or route, with identifying customer information removed?

**Listen for:** measurable, area-specific commitments; a clear distinction between estimate and guarantee; transparent capacity constraints; and evidence from recent operations.

### B. Order creation, pickup and chain of custody

- How can Nantty create a delivery: dashboard, API, mobile app, CSV, email, or manual dispatch? Which is production-ready today?
- What exact fields do you need to book a delivery? Can we minimize personal data and avoid sharing unnecessary buyer/seller information?
- How is a pickup window agreed with the seller? Can the seller reschedule or cancel before collection?
- What does the rider verify at pickup—order/reference number, package count, package condition, recipient, or seller handover code?
- Is there a pickup receipt with a timestamp and proof such as a one-time PIN, signature, or photo? Can the seller dispute a pickup record?
- How are parcels labeled and kept associated with the correct order throughout sorting and transfers?
- Can the courier collect from multiple sellers on a route? If yes, how do you prevent mix-ups and what are the added fees and timing effects?
- What packaging, labeling, weight, dimension, and prohibited-item rules apply?
- How do you handle fragile, perishable, high-value, oversized, or restricted items? Which categories should be excluded from the pilot?
- Can sellers contact dispatch during an active pickup? What is the escalation path and response time?

### C. Tracking, delivery and proof

- What delivery statuses can we receive, and how quickly are they updated? Can you share the exact status list and definitions?
- Can Nantty and the buyer receive a tracking link or notifications by SMS/WhatsApp/email? Who pays messaging charges?
- Is rider location shared live, periodically, or not at all? What is the privacy and retention policy for location data?
- What counts as successful delivery? Is delivery only to the named recipient, or can a household member/representative accept it?
- What proof of delivery is captured: recipient PIN, signature, name, timestamp, GPS, or photo? Can we choose a privacy-conscious method?
- How do you prevent a parcel being marked delivered before handoff? How can a buyer report “not received” and how quickly is evidence reviewed?
- Can delivery instructions be added without exposing sensitive information to people who do not need it?
- How are buyer contact details protected, and are riders prohibited from using them for unrelated contact or marketing?

### D. Failed, delayed, cancelled and returned deliveries

- What are the exact status and process when the buyer is unreachable, the address is incorrect, the buyer refuses the parcel, the seller is unavailable, or a rider cannot access the location?
- How many delivery attempts are included in the quoted fee? How long does the rider wait at the address, and what happens after that?
- Who can authorize a reattempt, address change, cancellation, return-to-seller, or reroute? What are the cutoffs?
- What are the fees for failed attempts, waiting, storage, address changes, cancellation after pickup, and return-to-seller? Ask for the full schedule in writing.
- Where is an undelivered parcel held, who can access it, and what is the maximum holding period before return or another action?
- What is the reverse-logistics process and cost? Can a buyer return an item through the same courier, and how is the seller notified?
- How do you handle a parcel that is lost, damaged, opened, tampered with, or short at delivery? What evidence and claim deadline are required?
- What happens if a parcel is delayed beyond the promised window? Is there an escalation SLA, service credit, or other remedy?
- Who communicates with the buyer and seller during an exception, and can Nantty see the same case/ticket history?

### E. Pricing and commercial terms

Ask for a written rate card, not only a verbal estimate.

- What is the price by area, distance band, parcel size/weight, service level, and pickup/drop-off type?
- Is the price per order, per parcel, per stop, or per attempted delivery? How are multi-item orders priced?
- Which surcharges apply: fuel, remote area, waiting, failed attempt, return, insurance, weekend, peak period, or cash collection?
- Are taxes included? Are there minimum monthly fees, deposits, onboarding fees, or volume commitments?
- When and how are invoices issued? What supporting delivery-level records accompany each invoice? What is the dispute window?
- What are payment terms, accepted payment methods, and consequences of a billing dispute?
- Can rates change during the pilot? What notice is given, and can Nantty stop the pilot without penalty?
- Can you provide a sample invoice with every fee shown?
- Can delivery charges be shown separately to the buyer or seller, with no unapproved deductions from order proceeds?

### F. Liability, insurance, safety and compliance

- Which legal entity would contract with Nantty? Can you provide company registration details and the authorized signatory?
- What insurance covers goods while in your custody and in transit? What are the limits, exclusions, excess/deductible, and claims process?
- What is the maximum declared value you accept? How are high-value parcels handled?
- Which item types are excluded or require special handling? How are dangerous or prohibited goods screened?
- How are riders recruited, identified, trained, supervised, and supported? What happens after a safety incident or complaint?
- Are riders employees, contractors, or third-party fleet partners? Who is accountable for their conduct and parcel custody?
- What is the incident escalation contact and after-hours contact?
- What security controls protect shipment data? Who can access it, where is it stored, how long is it retained, and how can it be deleted?
- Do you use subcontractors or onward carriers? If so, where, and does your liability and evidence chain continue through them?
- Can you provide your standard service agreement, privacy terms, insurance certificate, prohibited-items policy, and claims policy before any pilot begins?

Do not treat a verbal assurance as proof of insurance, licensing, data safeguards, or liability. Have the relevant documents reviewed before signing.

### G. Technology and support

- Is there a documented API or webhook that is available now? Can you show a live sandbox/demo and sample payloads?
- Are delivery creation and status events idempotent, timestamped, and traceable to a stable shipment ID?
- How are duplicate bookings, API outages, and status delays detected and corrected?
- Is there a dashboard for dispatch, exceptions, proof of pickup/delivery, and invoice reconciliation?
- What are support hours, response targets, and escalation contacts for urgent active deliveries?
- Can we start with a controlled manual process for a small pilot and automate only after the workflow is proven?

**Pilot principle:** Do not make the launch depend on an API that has only been promised. Start with a secure, auditable workflow the partner can operate reliably today, then assess integration after the pilot.

## 3. Recommended pilot proposal

Offer this as a starting point and adjust to their real capacity. The goal is to learn safely without making a long-term commitment.

### Scope

- **Duration:** 2–4 weeks after a short setup and test period.
- **Initial volume:** 50 delivery attempts total, with a review checkpoint after the first 10. Increase only if the checkpoint is passed.
- **Merchants:** 5–10 sellers who can reliably package orders and meet pickup windows.
- **Coverage:** One compact, mutually agreed service area; add a second only if route density and performance justify it.
- **Delivery types:** Standard door-to-door parcels only. Exclude cash handling, high-value goods, perishables, restricted goods, oversized parcels, and any category without agreed handling and insurance.
- **Booking:** Use the partner’s proven current channel (dashboard/app or a controlled booking sheet). Assign one Nantty operations contact and one partner dispatch contact.
- **Payments:** Keep marketplace goods payment and escrow decisions separate from courier charges. Record each delivery fee and any return fee explicitly; do not let delivery status alone release or refund marketplace funds.
- **No exclusivity:** Either party may pause the pilot for safety, service, data, or commercial concerns. No minimum-volume commitment beyond the agreed test.

### Staged rollout and safety gates

1. **Setup:** Confirm the service area, parcel limits, pricing, contacts, escalation path, data fields, and exception/return process in writing.
2. **Test run:** Run 3–5 test deliveries with consenting participants and low-value parcels. Validate pickup confirmation, tracking, proof of delivery, and failure handling.
3. **First checkpoint:** Review the first 10 real delivery attempts. Continue only if parcels are traceable, charges match the quote, exceptions are handled, and no unresolved safety or custody issue exists.
4. **Pilot:** Complete up to 50 attempts over 2–4 weeks, with a weekly operations review.
5. **Decision:** Compare actual performance and cost with the success criteria below. Expand, revise, or stop based on evidence.

### Suggested success measures

Agree the definitions and targets with the partner before launch. These are proposed pilot thresholds, not claims about current capability.

- At least **90%** delivered within the agreed service window.
- At least **95%** of orders have complete pickup and delivery status/evidence records.
- **100%** of completed deliveries have acceptable proof of delivery.
- At least **90%** successful first delivery attempts (measure failures separately by cause).
- **Zero** unresolved lost, damaged, tampered, or misdelivered parcels at pilot close.
- **100%** of invoice line items match agreed rates or have a documented, approved exception.
- Buyer and seller support contacts receive an initial response within the mutually agreed time.
- Record actual cost per successful delivery, cost per attempt, return rate, delivery time, complaints, and reasons for failure.

If the sample is too small for a percentage to be meaningful, report both the numerator and denominator (for example, 9/10 within window), not just the percentage.

## 4. What to observe during the meeting

### Strong signals

- They can describe a real parcel’s full journey and show the evidence generated at each handoff.
- They know their service map and route capacity, and are candid about areas they cannot serve.
- They provide a complete, understandable rate card and explain failed delivery and return fees upfront.
- They have a named operations owner, escalation route, claims process, and measurable service data.
- They can show a real dashboard/app, tracking example, sample proof of delivery, and invoice.
- They distinguish a current capability from a roadmap item and are comfortable starting small.
- They treat personal data, rider safety, package custody, and customer complaints as operational priorities.

### Warning signs / reasons to pause

- “We deliver everywhere” without a map, service-level data, or clear exclusions.
- Refusal to disclose fees until after dispatch, or vague/variable fees without approval controls.
- No reliable shipment ID, no pickup receipt, weak proof of delivery, or inability to investigate a disputed handoff.
- Claims of guaranteed delivery with no written scope, remedies, or exception process.
- No written explanation of loss/damage liability or insurance, or unclear use of subcontractors.
- Pressure for exclusivity, a large minimum volume, a long contract, or a deposit before a small test.
- They expect Nantty to share more customer data than is operationally necessary.
- They suggest that courier delivery automatically proves buyer acceptance or should automatically decide escrow/refund disputes.
- No named person who owns active delivery issues.

## 6. After-meeting checklist

- Send a concise recap of what was agreed, what remains unconfirmed, and each owner/date.
- Request the partner’s written rate card, sample invoice, standard contract, privacy terms, insurance evidence, claims policy, service map, and status/proof-of-delivery examples.
- Select the pilot area based on confirmed coverage, seller density, buyer demand, and support capacity—not broad geographic claims.
- Choose participating sellers and explain pickup cutoffs, packaging, excluded goods, delivery expectations, and exception contacts.
- Define how buyers are notified, how delivery proof is stored, how disputes are escalated, and how location/contact data is limited.
- Test the complete flow before real orders: booking, pickup, status updates, recipient handoff, failed attempt, cancellation, and return to seller.
- Confirm pricing and operational terms in writing before the first paid delivery. Keep pilot data and fees auditable.
- Review performance weekly and stop or narrow the pilot if there is a serious custody, safety, privacy, or billing issue.
