# Platform benchmark: TikTok, Meta, Twitch, X and Kick (October 2026)

**Draft, not legal advice.** This is a working comparison prepared on 2026-10-17 and rechecked against the code on 2026-10-21 to improve Nantty's legal drafts (all version 1.0, draft, `isReviewedByLegal: false`). Nothing here has been reviewed by a lawyer. Every summary of another platform is written in our own words from their public pages; none of their wording is reused. Their documents change often, so re-check a page before relying on it.

> Historical fee note: the marketplace-fee row reflects the proposal at the time of comparison and is superseded by the founder's later 0% sales-commission policy. Actual payment-provider processing and withdrawal charges are passed through at cost. This document is retained as research history, not current fee copy or legal terms.

## 1. Scope and method

We compared only the parts of the five platforms' terms and privacy documents that cover things Nantty actually does: feed posts, clips, stories, lives with gifts and coins, Nantty Points, the Creator Program (gifts, tips, ads revenue share, Premium pool, fan subscriptions), affiliates, the marketplace (sellers, buyers, escrow, refunds, disputes, seller-set shipping), ads and boosts (ad balance and ad credit), checkmarks and Premium, private accounts and follow requests, end-to-end encrypted chats and group chats, mentions, moderation and appeals, copyright, accounts, guests and deletion, biometric KYC, and the data, sharing, transfer, retention, rights and cookie rules for those features.

We left out features Nantty does not have, such as dating, banking-style wallets, crypto and NFTs, vehicle and housing listings, VR, AI chatbots, child-specific messaging apps, ticketing, fundraisers, jobs, Twitch Turbo/Prime and gambling streams.

Each Nantty claim was then checked against the code and the Help Centre (`services/typescript/support/content/help`). Where they disagree, the mismatch is listed in section 6 rather than hidden.

## 4. Where Nigerian law changes the picture

These are the places where copying a foreign platform's approach would mean something different, or be unsafe, in Nigeria.

- **Foreign forum and law (TikTok: Singapore arbitration; X: Texas courts; Kick: London arbitration).** FCCPA 2018 protects Nigerian consumers from unfair terms (ss.120–127) and keeps the FCCPC's jurisdiction, which a contract cannot remove. Nigerian law with Lagos-seated arbitration under the Arbitration and Mediation Act 2023, plus small claims and regulators, is the safer model. Nantty already does this.
- **"No refunds" and keeping fees on refunds.** FCCPA ss.120–122 require goods that are defective or not as described to be repaired, replaced or refunded, and blanket no-refund terms are risky. The owner rule to keep the escrow and processing fees after a post-payout refund needs counsel review, because the buyer ends up with less than the full price for a faulty item. The draft keeps the buyer's full rights against the seller.
- **Coins, ad balance and stored value (CBN).** Foreign platforms treat virtual currency as a licence, not money. In Nigeria, anything that holds value redeemable for cash can look like e-money under CBN rules. Coins are safe while they can never be cashed out by the buyer; creator conversion runs through the licensed partner. Ad balance is withdrawable, so it is closer to stored value; counsel should confirm it sits inside the payment partner's licence.
- **Minors (NDPA s.31, GAID).** Platforms that let 13–17s join with a tick-box (TikTok's Nigeria supplement, Kick) rely on parental consent that NDPA says must be verified appropriately. Nantty states the gap honestly; it must be closed or the age raised.
- **Biometrics (NDPA s.30, sensitive data).** X and Meta use biometric ID checks with consent. Under the NDPA biometric data is sensitive, needs explicit consent and a DPIA. Nantty records the consent version and stores no templates or images, which is stronger.
- **Cross-border transfers (NDPA ss.41–43, GAID).** TikTok relies on "necessary for the contract" for Nigeria. GAID expects a recorded basis per transfer; Nantty's per-provider table supports this but has blanks.
- **Takedown and liaison (NITDA Code of Practice).** Large platforms must remove unlawful content on notice from authorised agencies and non-consensual intimate images within 24 hours, have a Nigerian presence and a liaison officer. Foreign terms do not mention this; Nantty's do.
- **Cybercrimes Act 2015 (as amended 2024).** Traffic data retention of 2 years (s.38) and 72-hour incident reporting apply to service providers. The retention table includes it; counsel to confirm it applies.
- **Copyright Act 2022.** The US DMCA model (used by Twitch, X and Kick) is not the Nigerian law. Nantty follows ss.54–58, including the s.56 warning-then-suspension path.
- **ARCON.** Boosts, sponsored ads, Promoted comment ads and creator paid partnerships may need pre-exposure approval. Foreign platforms rely on FTC-style disclosure only.

## 5. Best practices, strengths and risks

### Adopted in the 1.0 draft

1. Coins described as a licence with a clear list of what they cannot be used for (Twitch Bits policy, Kick KICKs policy), and gift and coin misuse added to the Community Guidelines.
2. A published creator split, with tiers, instead of an undisclosed formula (Kick's flat split is the model; TikTok and X do not publish theirs).
3. Freeze-then-deduct style handling of store refunds on coins, stated as it actually works (TikTok).
4. Reversal rules stated per product: subscriptions reversed, gifts not (Meta Stars, Twitch).
5. Notice before lowering creator rates and no retroactive changes (Twitch Monetized Streamer Agreement).
6. Price-change notice that applies from the next billing period (X, Twitch).
7. Clear checkmark meaning: never bought on its own, refund rules on rejection, re-check on name change (Meta Verified, X's distinction between paid and verified).
8. E2EE reporting that sends only the messages the reporter selects, and honest disclosure of what is not encrypted (Meta's model, avoiding X's weak key backup claims).
9. Statement of reasons and strike removal on successful appeal (Meta, TikTok EEA terms).
10. A per-category retention table and deletion timeline (Meta, X).
11. A per-provider transfer table (GAID, TikTok's Nigeria supplement).
12. Consent and policy version records explained to users.
13. Guests and private accounts explained in the Terms.

### Where Nantty does better

- Nigerian law and a Lagos seat, with small claims and regulators kept open, instead of Singapore, Texas or London.
- Money is never forfeited on closure or ban (Meta, Twitch and TikTok forfeit unused items or balances).
- No third-party ad networks, analytics SDKs, contact upload or data sales; advertisers get aggregated results only.
- A narrow content licence that ends after deletion, with no AI training on chats (X and TikTok take much wider rights).
- A fixed, published ranking boost for checkmarks and staff unable to push content.
- Automation never bans on its own, and every permanent ban is reviewed by a person.
- Affiliate commissions only on completed orders, locked at checkout.

### Risks

- Keeping fees after a post-payout refund (FCCPA).
- Parental consent not implemented while under-18s can sign up (NDPA s.31).
- Ad balance as possible stored value; undelivered ad spend returning as non-withdrawable ad credit.
- Policy promises that the code does not yet keep (section 6), which could be treated as misleading under FCCPA s.123-type rules on false representations. Counsel should verify the exact FCCPA section.
- No CSAM reporting pipeline at launch.

## 7. Platform improvements (product changes for the owner to decide)

1. Parental consent and age assurance for 13–17s, or raise the minimum age (NDPA s.31). Also decide 18+ for buying coins, gifting and going live.
2. **Done in code (2026-10-19).** Undelivered escrows now move to staff review at payment + 30 days instead of auto-releasing. Finish the staff queue and the notice that contacts both parties.
3. **Done in code (2026-10-19).** Post-payout refunds recover only the seller's portion and keep Nantty's fees. Counsel still needs to confirm the FCCPA position, and the Help articles must distinguish refunds before release from refunds after release.
4. Handle web card chargebacks on coin purchases (debit coins, record coin debt) and decide whether coin debt is collected.
5. A tool to hold or reverse creator earnings from a specific invalid gift.
6. Automatic monetisation pause at the strike threshold, with notice and appeal.
7. CSAM hash matching with a vetted provider and a reporting pipeline (NCMEC CyberTipline and Nigerian authorities).
8. Ad balance withdrawal fee and destination to match the owner rule, and refund undelivered ad-balance spend as ad balance, not credit.
9. Ad load for Gold business accounts to match the owner rule.
10. One-offer-per-account limit, if the owner keeps that rule.
11. Retention periods for live recordings, KYC documents and shared locations.
12. A transparency report (removals, appeals, government requests), as Meta and Twitch publish.
13. A public ad library for political and issue ads, if political ads are allowed.
14. Paystack: resolved — owner confirmed Flutterwave is the only payment provider (2026-10-17); no Paystack work.
15. A separate Virtual Items Policy page (new document key) once coins, gifts and Nantty Points rules settle, as TikTok and Kick do.
