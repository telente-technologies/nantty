# Data Processing Agreement

This Data Processing Agreement (the "**DPA**") forms part of the <mark>[name of main agreement]</mark> dated <mark>[date]</mark> (the "**Agreement**") between <mark>[Nantty legal entity name]</mark>, RC <mark>[RC number]</mark>, of <mark>[address]</mark> (the "**Controller**"), and <mark>[Processor name]</mark>, of <mark>[address]</mark> (the "**Processor**").

## 1. Definitions

Terms such as "personal data", "data subject", "processing", "data controller", "data processor", "sensitive personal data" and "personal data breach" have the meanings in the NDPA.

- "**Data Protection Law**": the NDPA, the GAID and any NDPC regulations or directives, and any other law in Annex 3 that applies to the processing.
- "**Sub-processor**": a third party the Processor engages to process Controller personal data.
- "**Restricted Transfer**": a transfer of Controller personal data outside Nigeria, or out of the UK or EU where Annex 3 applies.

## 2. Roles and instructions

2.1 The Controller determines the purposes and means of processing. The Processor processes Controller personal data only on the Controller's documented instructions, including this DPA and the Agreement, as described in Annex 1, unless the law requires otherwise (in which case it will tell the Controller first, unless the law forbids it).

2.2 The Processor will tell the Controller at once if it believes an instruction breaks Data Protection Law.

2.3 The Processor will not sell personal data, use it for its own purposes, combine it with other data except as needed for the Services, or use it to train, fine-tune or improve any artificial intelligence or machine-learning model unless the Controller agrees in writing.

## 3. Processor obligations (NDPA section 29)

The Processor will:

1. implement the technical and organisational measures in Annex 2, appropriate to the risk, including encryption, access control and resilience (NDPA section 39);
2. ensure that everyone authorised to process the data is bound by confidentiality;
3. help the Controller meet its obligations on security, breach notification, data protection impact assessments (NDPA section 28), consultation with the NDPC, and data subject rights;
4. keep a record of processing activities and make it available to the Controller and the NDPC on request;
5. if it is itself a data controller or processor of major importance, register with the NDPC and file its compliance audit returns as the GAID requires, and give the Controller evidence on request;
6. cooperate with the NDPC and other regulators.

## 4. Personal data breaches

4.1 The Processor will notify the Controller without undue delay, and in any case within <mark>24 hours</mark>, after becoming aware of a personal data breach affecting Controller personal data, so that the Controller can notify the NDPC within 72 hours as section 40 of the NDPA requires.

4.2 The notice will describe, as far as known: the nature of the breach, the categories and approximate number of data subjects and records, likely consequences, measures taken or proposed, and a contact point. The Processor will provide updates as more is known, take reasonable steps to contain and fix the breach, and not notify data subjects or regulators about Controller data without the Controller's approval unless the law requires.

4.3 Where the breach is also a cyber incident, the Processor will support the Controller in reporting to the national CERT within the 72 hours required by the Cybercrimes (Prohibition, Prevention, etc.) (Amendment) Act 2024.

## 5. Data subject rights

The Processor will, taking into account the nature of processing, help the Controller respond to requests from data subjects under sections 34 to 38 of the NDPA within the time the law allows. If the Processor receives a request directly, it will pass it to the Controller within <mark>2 business days</mark> and not respond except as instructed.

## 6. Sub-processors

6.1 The Controller generally authorises the Sub-processors listed in Annex 4. The Processor will give the Controller at least <mark>30</mark> days' notice of any new or replacement Sub-processor. If the Controller objects on reasonable data protection grounds within <mark>15</mark> days, the parties will discuss in good faith; if they cannot agree, the Controller may end the affected Services and receive a refund of prepaid fees for them.

6.2 The Processor will impose on each Sub-processor written obligations no less protective than this DPA and remains fully liable for its Sub-processors.

## 7. International transfers

7.1 The Processor will not make a Restricted Transfer except as permitted by sections 41 to 43 of the NDPA and the GAID, and Annex 3 where it applies. The parties rely on <mark>[an adequacy decision of the NDPC / these contractual clauses as an appropriate safeguard / binding corporate rules / another basis]</mark>. The Processor will give the Controller the information needed to record the transfer and its basis.

7.2 If a transfer basis becomes invalid, the parties will agree an alternative promptly, and the Controller may suspend the transfer until they do.

## 8. Audits

The Processor will make available all information needed to show compliance with this DPA and allow audits by the Controller, its licensed Data Protection Compliance Organisation (DPCO) or the NDPC, no more than once a year unless there is a breach or a regulator requires it, on 14 days' notice, during business hours and under confidentiality. An independent report (such as ISO 27001 or SOC 2 Type II) less than 12 months old may be used instead for controls it covers.

## 9. Return and deletion

When the Services end, the Processor will, at the Controller's choice, return all Controller personal data in a common format and then delete it, or delete it, within <mark>30</mark> days, and confirm deletion in writing, unless the law requires it to keep some data, in which case it will keep it secure and use it only for that legal purpose.

## 10. Automated decisions

If the Services involve automated decision-making that significantly affects data subjects, the Processor will tell the Controller, explain the logic in meaningful terms, and help the Controller let data subjects obtain human review under section 37 of the NDPA.

## 11. Liability and precedence

Each party's liability under this DPA is subject to the limits in the Agreement, except that those limits do not reduce any party's liability to data subjects or regulators where the law forbids it. If this DPA conflicts with the Agreement on data protection, this DPA prevails. If the Annex 3 transfer clauses conflict with this DPA, those clauses prevail for that transfer.

## 12. Governing law

This DPA is governed by the laws of the Federal Republic of Nigeria, except that the transfer clauses in Annex 3 are governed by the law they specify. Disputes are resolved as in the Agreement.

## Annex 2: Security measures

The Processor maintains at least: a named security owner and written information security policy; risk assessment and annual review; encryption in transit (TLS 1.2+) and at rest; role-based, least-privilege access with unique IDs, multi-factor authentication and prompt removal of leavers; secure password storage (salted hashing); audit logging and monitoring; network segmentation, firewalls and intrusion detection; vulnerability scanning and patching; secure development and change management; incident response procedures; business continuity and backups with tested restores; physical security of facilities; secure disposal of media; and staff training.

## Annex 3: Additional jurisdictions

- **UK and EU.** For Restricted Transfers of personal data subject to the EU GDPR, the Standard Contractual Clauses adopted by Commission Implementing Decision (EU) 2021/914 (Module 2 controller-to-processor, or Module 3 processor-to-processor) are incorporated, with Clause 7 not used, Option 2 in Clause 9 with the notice period in clause 6.1, the optional wording in Clause 11 not used, and Clauses 17 and 18 governed by the law and courts of <mark>Ireland</mark>. For UK data, the ICO International Data Transfer Addendum (version B1.0) applies with the same details. The Processor will meet Article 28 GDPR, which this DPA mirrors.
- **United States.** Where US state privacy laws (such as the CCPA) apply, the Processor is a "service provider", "contractor" or "processor" and will not sell or share personal information, retain, use or disclose it outside the direct business relationship, or combine it with other data except as those laws allow; it certifies that it understands these restrictions.
- **Ghana.** The Processor will comply with the Data Protection Act 2012 (Act 843), including the security measures in it, and support the Controller's registration with the Data Protection Commission.
- **Kenya.** The Processor will comply with the Data Protection Act 2019 and its regulations, be registered with the ODPC where required, and help the Controller answer access requests within 7 days and notify breaches within 72 hours.
- **South Africa.** The Processor is an "operator" under section 21 of POPIA, will process only with the Controller's knowledge or authorisation, treat the information as confidential, and notify the Controller immediately of any security compromise.
