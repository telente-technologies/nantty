## Report content that uses your work

If someone posts your music, video or other creative work without permission, open the post, clip or sound menu and choose **Report copyright**. You need to be signed in.

The form asks for:

- your full legal name and contact details (and your company or label, if you act for one)
- the original work: a link, or an upload
- proof that you own it, such as a registration certificate, a distributor or release page, or a contract
- when your work appears in the content (start and end times)
- two declarations: that you believe in good faith the use isn't allowed, and that the information is accurate and you are the owner or authorised to act for them
- your electronic signature: type your full legal name

Knowingly false claims can have legal consequences, and we may stop accepting claims from people who misuse the form.

## What happens when a claim is upheld

Our copyright team reviews every claim. An audio fingerprint match can help them, but a person always decides, based on the proof.

### If the claim is about the audio

We keep the video up and replace its soundtrack with silence. The sound can no longer be reused, and other clips that used the same sound are muted too. Audio claims don't add a strike. The uploader is told why and how to send a counter-notice.

### If the claim is about the video itself

The uploader gets a warning, in the app and by email, to remove the content within **48 hours**. The post shows a countdown to its owner.

- Remove it before the deadline: no strike. The warning stays on your record for 180 days.
- Still up at the deadline: we take it down automatically and add a **copyright strike**.

Claims about live videos are taken down straight away.

## Strikes and your account status

Open **Settings > Account status** to see where you stand:

- **Good standing**: no active strikes and no pending deadlines.
- **At risk**: you have an active strike, or a takedown deadline is running.
- **Restricted**: you have 3 active copyright strikes within 90 days. Earning money (gifts, ads revenue share, subscriptions) and going live are paused, and your account is reviewed for suspension.

Each strike expires after 90 days. Your account status page lists every warning and strike with the reason, the affected content and the expiry date. Studio and the seller dashboard show a summary too. These limits can change; the page always shows the current ones.

## Counter-notices

If you believe your content was muted or removed by mistake, for example you own the work, have a licence, or your use is allowed by law, send a counter-notice from your Account status page. Include a statement explaining why and an email address we can share with the claimant.

If our team accepts your counter-notice, we tell the claimant. Unless they tell us within **10 business days** that they've started court action against you, we restore your content: the original sound comes back, or the video is visible again, and any strike or warning from that claim is removed.

## Appeals

You can appeal any active strike or warning from your Account status page. A different reviewer looks at it. If your appeal succeeds, the strike is removed straight away.
